## Wedding budget

Recurring demand is explicit across The Knot, WeddingWire, and Zola. These sites expose budget planners/trackers and repeatedly place budget setting near the start of the wedding-planning workflow.

Examples of supported query families: `wedding budget`, `wedding budget calculator`, `wedding budget template`, `wedding budget breakdown`.

## Wedding checklist

The Knot, WeddingWire, and Zola each publish or operate dedicated wedding-planning checklists. The demand family recurs as both interactive planning tools and downloadable/printable resources.

Examples: `wedding checklist`, `wedding planning checklist`, `wedding checklist printable`, `wedding checklist timeline`.

## Wedding timeline

The Knot and Zola both publish month-by-month wedding-planning timelines, and checklist products adapt tasks to the wedding date.

Examples: `wedding timeline`, `wedding planning timeline`, `wedding day timeline`, `12 month wedding timeline`.

## Guest list

The Knot, WeddingWire, and Zola repeatedly expose guest-list management as a core planning job connected to addresses, RSVPs, meal choices and seating.

Examples: `wedding guest list`, `guest list manager`, `wedding guest list template`.

## Seating chart

WeddingWire has a dedicated wedding seating-chart tool; The Knot and Zola also repeatedly place seating-chart creation after RSVP/guest-list finalization.

Examples: `wedding seating chart`, `seating chart maker`, `wedding seating chart template`.

## Wedding vows

The Knot's planning hub and Zola's planning checklist both treat writing wedding vows/processional ceremony content as a recurring planning task rather than an isolated article topic.

Examples: `wedding vows examples`, `wedding vows template`, `wedding vow generator`.

## Wedding website

The Knot, WeddingWire, and Zola each make a wedding website a first-class wedding-planning product used for schedules, guest information, RSVPs, registry links and logistics.

Examples: `wedding website`, `wedding website examples`, `wedding website FAQ`, `wedding website wording`.

## Wedding registry

The Knot and Zola both treat registry creation/management as a core wedding-planning job integrated with wedding websites and guest communication.

Examples: `wedding registry`, `wedding registry checklist`, `wedding registry ideas`.

## Wedding venue

Venue discovery and booking recur across The Knot, WeddingWire and Zola as one of the earliest planning decisions; related content/tool families include finding venues, comparing venues and estimating costs.

Examples: `wedding venue`, `wedding venue finder`, `wedding venue checklist`, `wedding venue cost`.

## Wedding vendor

Vendor discovery/management recurs across The Knot, WeddingWire and Zola, covering photographers, caterers, florists, DJs and other service providers.

Examples: `wedding vendor`, `wedding vendor checklist`, `wedding vendor questions`, `wedding vendor manager`.

## Wedding RSVP

The Knot and Zola both maintain dedicated RSVP guidance and online RSVP workflows; WeddingWire integrates RSVPs with its guest-list/wedding-website planning tools.

Examples: `wedding rsvp`, `wedding rsvp wording`, `wedding rsvp reminder`, `online wedding rsvp`.

## Wedding invitation wording

The Knot, Zola and Greenvelope independently publish dedicated wedding-invitation-wording guides with formal/casual variants, family/host variants, RSVP wording and reception-only variants.

Examples: `wedding invitation wording`, `formal wedding invitation wording`, `casual wedding invitation wording`, `wedding invitation wording examples`.

## Wedding dress code

The Knot maintains dedicated wedding dress-code wording guidance and both invitation/website guidance repeatedly treat attire wording as a recurring guest-communication problem. Greenvelope also includes dress-code wording in its invitation structure.

Examples: `wedding dress code`, `wedding dress code wording`, `wedding website dress code`, `wedding invitation dress code wording`.

## Evidence interpretation

The roots above are promoted to `verified/L2` because recurring demand is observable in real SERPs and/or first-party site structures across established wedding sites. This is root-level evidence only. It does not substitute for downstream keyword-level Volume, KD, CPC, KGR, trend, or SERP-competition validation.
